Research

I develop statistical methodology for dependent functional data: curves and multivariate series that are irregularly observed, associated across components, and often tied to a rare event. Wearable movement and solar–terrestrial measurements are different sciences. They lead to the same kinds of questions.

  • Representation. How should an irregular trajectory be described so that its shape is retained?
  • Dependence. How should association across components, and changes in that association, enter the analysis?
  • Interpretation and uncertainty. How should a forecast be read, and its uncertainty reported, when the predictors are dependent and the event of interest is rare?

My doctoral research studied these questions for irregular functional observations, with wearable records of human movement as the motivating setting. In related work with Prof. Siu Hung Cheung, I studied designs for trials with ordinal outcomes, and with clinical collaborators the statistical validation of a screening questionnaire.

My postdoctoral research carries the same questions to solar–terrestrial series, where several instruments record related signals and the events of interest are rare. The direction I am developing is methodological: representation, dependence, and uncertainty for this class of data.
